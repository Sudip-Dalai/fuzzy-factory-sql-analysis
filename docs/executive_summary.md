# Executive Summary

**Subject: Growth is real, but it leans heavily on one channel and one product**

**Bottom line:** Between March 2012 and March 2015, Maven Fuzzy Factory grew to $1.94M in revenue across 32,313 orders. The growth is not just more traffic. Visitors also became much more likely to buy: conversion rose from about 3% in 2012 to about 8% in early 2015, and revenue per visit rose from $1.60 to about $5.20.

## Where we're exposed

- **One channel:** Google non-brand search drove about 56% of revenue in 2014, down from 67.5% in 2012 but still over half.
- **One product:** The Original Mr. Fuzzy brings in 62.5% of revenue.
- **Little repeat business:** only about 2% of 2014 customers ordered again within 90 days.

## What I'd do next

1. **Review the Socialbook pilot before spending more.** It converted 1.08% of 5,095 visits and earned $0.73 per visit, versus 6.7% to 8.9% for the search channels. There is no spend data, so return on investment cannot be calculated, only that performance is weak.
2. **Look into Birthday Sugar Panda refunds.** It has the best margin (68.5%) but the highest refund rate (6.04%), about 1 in 17 items sent back.
3. **Test a follow-up message about a month after first purchase.** Of customers who do reorder, about half do so within 30 days and 98% within 90. This is a hypothesis to test, not a proven fix.

## Caveats

The first and last months are partial. The analysis shows that conversion improved, not why. Refunds for the most recent orders may not be fully recorded yet. Every repeat order in the data falls within 107 days of the first, which looks short for a three-year window and was not investigated.
